The Hall of Design · Exhibit 1

A compass that needs a clock

Every autumn, eastern monarchs fly up to about 4,000 km to a few fir forests in central Mexico. The ones that make the trip have never been there.

They steer by the sun. But the sun moves across the sky all day, so keeping a fixed angle to it would bend the route. Researchers found a body clock in their antennae that corrects the angle hour by hour.

with the clockwithout the clock
Press a button to send a butterfly south.

The flight here is simplified, but the point is the real one: the compass is useless without the clock, and the clock is useless without the compass.

The Hall of Design · Exhibit 2

A warning, sent and received

When some trees are attacked by insects, they release chemicals into the air. Neighbouring trees that pick these up begin building their own defences before the insects arrive.

Under the ground, fine fungal threads wrap around the roots and join one tree to the next. Scientists are still studying how much passes along them.

One tree is marked. Send in the caterpillars, then watch it and its neighbours.

A warning only helps if something is ready to receive it. Both sides have to be there.

Psalm 104:16 · Isaiah 55:12.
The Hall of Design · Exhibit 3

A dance that is a map

A bee that finds flowers comes home and dances on the upright comb. The angle of her waggle run from straight up is the direction of the flowers from the sun. How long she waggles tells the others how far.

Drag the flowers round the meadow and watch her dance change.

The dance is only a map because the other bees can read it. Karl von Frisch won a Nobel Prize in 1973 for decoding it.

Case JM-00 · The Maker's Mark

Three things that only work when everything works

In each exhibit in this hall, an ability depends on several parts being in place at the same time. Take one away and the whole thing fails.

Visit all three. Each one has something to try. As you go, ask the question an investigator would ask: could this have come together a piece at a time, by accident, or does it look made?

All three exhibits examined

What the hall shows

A compass that only works with a clock. Trees that send a warning and trees that act on it. A dance that only means something to bees that can read it.

In each case, the parts are useless on their own. That is what a maker's mark looks like.
