## A word before you generate these

This is the one case in the archive where the artwork carries something other
than evidence, and it is worth being deliberate about it.

- **Nothing gruesome, and no bodies on crosses.** Every prompt below shows the
  place, the object or the aftermath. A Year 7 class will be looking at these
  on a screen with no warning, and the case does not need horror to land — the
  ledger does that work.
- **No faces, and no depiction of Jesus.** Partly the house style (no people
  unless asked for), partly because a drawn face invites a class to argue about
  the face instead of the file. The empty middle cross says more.
- **Restraint reads as weight here.** The temptation on this case is golden
  light and rays. Resist it. The rest of the archive is a quiet, dusty,
  evidence-room world, and this case is more powerful for being in the same
  room as the others rather than suddenly in a stained-glass window.

## Before you hand the folder back

- **Every plaque comes out blank.** The labels are typeset afterwards as real SVG
  text, measured to the plaque. That is why they read crisply at any zoom, and why
  they can be re-labelled later — in te reo Māori, say — without regenerating art.
- **3:2 landscape**, 1536 × 1024, for everything except the seal.
- **Say "no text" twice**, and for the ledger plates say "illegible scribble"
  as well. Image models desperately want to write real words on an account page,
  and a legible number on a ledger will start an argument in class about what it
  says.
- **Check the crosses come out empty.** This is the one prompt in the archive
  where a model is likely to add a figure you did not ask for. If it does, add
  *"the crosses are bare and empty, no figures at all"* and generate again.
- Save the files in this folder under the names given above, then tell Claude the
  folder is ready.
